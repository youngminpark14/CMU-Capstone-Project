# From the checkpoints to the repository

This page traces each of the six capstone checkpoints, and the instructors' feedback on each, to
the code. For each item it names a file to open and, where one exists, a test that shows it, and
says whether the item was kept, changed or dropped.

| Checkpoint | Title | What it proposed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 | Prediction Market Forecasting and Trading Agent | The problem, and an eight-step cycle from watching the news to recording outcomes. |
| 2.1 | Agent Design Refinement | A six-step reasoning loop, short-term and long-term memory, and the tools the loop calls. |
| 3.1 | RAG and Retrieval Design Integration | Three retrieval collections, a Federal Reserve demonstration, and failure modes. |
| 4.1 | Tree-of-Thought Integration Plan | A scenario tree searched with a beam in the forecasting step: scoring, pruning, budgets. |
| 5.1 | Multi-Agent Architecture and Coordination Plan | Seven agents, four coordination rules, and how they share state. |
| 6.1 | Safety Guardrails and Human Intervention Plan | Limits in code, risk scoring, monitoring, evaluation metrics, and the human's role. |

The specification, [spec.md](spec.md), turned the six into one design. Its section "Changes from
the checkpoint documents" lists twelve changes, which are decision records 0001 to 0012 in
[decisions/](decisions/README.md). The checkpoint documents themselves are not committed here. This
page paraphrases them, quotes at most a short phrase, and leaves out the names of the author and the
instructors. Where the checkpoints disagree with each other, it says which one the spec and the
code follow.

**Kept** means the code does what the checkpoint proposed. **Changed** means it does something
close, and the cell says what. **Dropped** means it is not done; the reasons are collected under
[Proposed in a checkpoint and not done here](#proposed-in-a-checkpoint-and-not-done-here). Test
names are functions in the test files linked in the same cell.

## Proposed in a checkpoint and not done here

- **News wires, a newspaper and open-web search as evidence** (1.1, 2.1, 3.1). Evidence is official
  sources only ([0011](decisions/0011-official-sources-only.md)), and the spec puts the others out
  of scope. A generic RSS or Atom feed source exists for the live modes, off by default and
  secondary only.
- **A live dashboard** (6.1). Monitoring is a generated report, `pmfa report-status`
  ([0009](decisions/0009-generated-reports.md)).
- **Autonomy with real money** (1.1, 2.1). Out of scope ([0010](decisions/0010-no-real-money.md));
  the stage ladder is kept as stored state and benchmarks.
- **Cancelling resting orders as a trading tool** (1.1, 2.1). Orders are immediate-or-cancel, so
  nothing rests to cancel ([0012](decisions/0012-ioc-limit-orders.md)); cancelling is used only by
  the halt.
- **The 0.03 probability floor** (4.1). Removed; the protected slot is the only tail rule
  ([0005](decisions/0005-protected-slot-only-tail-rule.md)).
- **Raising a category's floor when a pruned branch was the realized path** (4.1). There is no
  floor to raise; the pruning audit reports the share, and nothing changes automatically
  ([0025](decisions/0025-pruning-audit.md)).
- **The agent learning from the reviewer's overrides** (6.1). Overrides are logged with reason
  codes; nothing feeds them back. Not built.
- **Episodes for different markets running in parallel** (5.1). Not built; a live pass runs its
  episodes one after another.
- **The for-and-against debate** (1.1, 2.1, 3.1). Checkpoint 4.1 replaced it with the scenario
  tree. Its two queries remain, written by the Researcher.
- **Adjusting the Critic's probability by the category's calibration** (4.1). The Critic is shown
  up to five resolved forecasts as examples; code does not adjust its number. Not built.
- **Lowering confidence when evidence conflicts** (1.1). Confidence comes from coverage and the
  origin count only ([0022](decisions/0022-support-ties-confidence.md)); the Critic may ask for
  more evidence instead. Not built.
- **Agents calling tools, and reading and writing the shared state through MCP tools** (2.1, 4.1,
  5.1). Roles take one record and return one; code runs every retrieval, check and order
  ([0003](decisions/0003-record-only-roles.md)), and the controller, not the roles, writes tree
  nodes and rejections through the MCP state server ([0031](decisions/0031-state-transport.md)).
- **The checks as LangChain tools** (4.1). They are plain functions run before the Critic.
- **Confidence from the spread of critic scores** (4.1). Confidence is coverage times the origin
  count's share, halved when the budget ran out ([0022](decisions/0022-support-ties-confidence.md)).
- **A separate benchmark for unusual market conditions** (the feedback on 2.1). Not built.
- **A 90-second limit in backtests** (4.1). The spec applies the call budget alone in backtests;
  the seconds cap is enforced only in the live modes.
- **Automatic execution of small orders on markets without calibration** (6.1). Replaced by the
  auto conditions, as the feedback on 6.1 asked.
- **Shrinking estimates by the agent's own record** (2.1). The shrinkage factor is a fixed default
  per category; fitting it from the ledger is out of the spec's scope.
- **A stored "main argument" for each open position** (2.1). Not a separate record; the cited
  documents are hashed, and a changed source behind an open position halts the agent.
- **A risk-score factor for the meaning the agents understood** (6.1). Not built.
- **A count of trades traced to incomplete episodes** (6.1). Not built as a metric.

## Where the spec and a checkpoint differ in what they attribute

The spec says that advancing a stage "requires the checkpoint 6.1 benchmarks" and gives thresholds:
Brier skill above zero on 200 backtested markets, four shadow weeks with positive simulated P&L
after fees and drawdown under 10%, and 100 approved trades with under 15% overridden. Checkpoint 6.1
names measures (the Brier score against the market price at forecast time, P&L after fees against a
no-trade baseline, the gate rejection rate, trades traced to incomplete episodes, the no-trades,
overrides with a reason, and cost and seconds per episode) and gives no thresholds and no stage
ladder. Checkpoints 1.1 and 2.1 say only that autonomy follows a sufficient record, and the
feedback on 2.1 asked for clearer benchmarks. The thresholds are the spec's; the code implements
them ([benchmarks.py](../src/pmfa/evaluation/benchmarks.py)).
In the same way, the spec calls the protected slot the 5.1 rule. Checkpoint 5.1 says only that the
controller keeps the best three "plus any protected outlier scenarios"; the test of what is
protected (its removal flips the trade, moves the cost by more than one risk unit, or pushes a
market to 0 or 1) is the spec's, and checkpoint 4.1's risk section is the nearest checkpoint text.
The spec also names its third episode test after a 2.1 "phantom edge"; checkpoint 2.1 does not use
the phrase, and its closest step asks whether the disagreement with the price is "worth anything
after fees".
